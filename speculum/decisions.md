Memory rewrites the past. Once you know how things turned out, you remember having known all along; psychologists call this hindsight bias. A record written before the outcome is the only honest mirror of your judgment.

## Why it matters to the counterweight

After a dozen graded decisions, the palace can see whether your intuition or your analysis is better calibrated, and in which domains. The [polarity map](/speculum/polarity-map) is then tuned by evidence instead of by guesswork.

Each decision is graded on two separate axes, because a good decision can have a bad outcome, and the reverse:

| | Good outcome | Bad outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Good process** | Earned | Bad luck |
| **Poor process** | Luck: do not repeat the method | Lesson |

## How to record one

Tell Claude `Decide: …`. Claude opens a record in `speculum/decisions/` and asks for:

1. The options you are weighing.
2. What you expect to happen, and how confident you are, as a percentage.
3. How you feel about it right now.

Claude then makes the strongest case for an option you are not choosing, once. You decide. On the review date, the decision appears in your weekly review for grading.
