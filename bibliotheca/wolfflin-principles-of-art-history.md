## In three sentences
Wölfflin, the Swiss art historian who perfected the side-by-side slide comparison, turns that method into a system. He uses five pairs of opposite concepts to separate the classic art of the sixteenth century from the Baroque of the seventeenth. His thesis is that vision itself has a history: "not everything is possible at all times". Neither style is better, because they are two complete ways of seeing.

> **BG:** Вьолфлин сравнява Ренесанса и Барока чрез пет двойки понятия. Книгата учи не какво да мислим за изкуството, а как да го гледаме.

## Key ideas
1. **Linear → painterly** (*linear / malerisch*). Firm contour and tactile edges give way to light, mass and tone. Compare Dürer and Rembrandt.
2. **Plane → recession.** Figures in parallel layers give way to diagonals that pull into depth.
3. **Closed → open form** (*tektonisch / atektonisch*). A self-contained composition gives way to one the frame seems to cut.
4. **Multiplicity → unity.** Independent, separately legible parts give way to parts subordinated to one dominant motif.
5. **Absolute → relative clarity.** Everything shown completely gives way to deliberate obscurity and partial revelation.
6. **Form over biography.** His approach is associated with the phrase "art history without names". The pairs apply to buildings too, for example Bramante against Bernini.
