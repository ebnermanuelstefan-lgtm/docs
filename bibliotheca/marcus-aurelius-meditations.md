# Meditations, Marcus Aurelius

## Summary in three sentences
These are the private Greek notebooks of a Roman emperor, written on the Danube frontier during the Marcomannic Wars. Their real title, *To Himself*, describes them exactly. In twelve books Marcus repeats a few Stoic exercises: separate what is up to you from what is not, act justly within your part, and see your life "from above" as a brief point in the whole of nature. It is philosophy as daily discipline, not as a system, and Book I is a list of what he owes to each of his teachers.

> **BG:** Личните бележки на Марк Аврелий, писани на гръцки по време на походи. Упражнения в това да приемаш онова, което не зависи от теб, и да действаш справедливо в онова, което зависи.

## Key ideas
1. **What is up to us.** Judgments, intentions and responses are ours. Body, reputation and fortune are not. Disturbance comes from our opinion of things.
2. **The obstacle becomes the way (V.20).**
3. **The view from above.** Picture the whole of time and matter, and vanity becomes absurd and calm natural.
4. **Change is nature's law (IV.3, IV.36).** Accepting flux is living *kata physin*, according to nature.
5. **Morning preparation (II.1).** Expect to meet the meddling, the ungrateful and the arrogant. They act from ignorance, they are your kin, and they cannot harm you.
6. **The common good.** "What is not good for the hive is not good for the bee." (VI.54)

## Quotations
- "The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way." (V.20, Hays trans.)
- Nowhere can you withdraw into greater quiet than into your own soul. (IV.3, paraphrase)
