## In three sentences
A dozen or so cryptic lines, first attested in Arabic at the end of the *Kitāb sirr al-khalīqa* attributed to "Balīnūs" (pseudo-Apollonius). They reached Latin Europe in the twelfth century and became the charter of alchemy, and Newton translated them himself. Their core is the principle of correspondence, "as above, so below", together with an operation of separating the subtle from the gross and reuniting them. Keep the Tablet distinct from the Greek *Corpus Hermeticum*, which Ficino translated for Cosimo de' Medici in 1463.

> **BG:** Кратък загадъчен текст, приписван на Хермес Трисмегист. От него идва формулата „каквото горе, такова и долу“, идеята за съответствието между космоса и човека.

## Key ideas
1. **Correspondence.** What is below is like what is above, to accomplish the miracles of the one thing.
2. **One source.** All things come from the One by adaptation.
3. **The cosmic parents.** The Sun is the father, the Moon the mother, the wind carried it, and the earth is its nurse. Later alchemy renders these as sulphur and mercury, king and queen.
4. **Solve et coagula.** Separate earth from fire and the subtle from the gross, gently and with skill. It rises to heaven and descends again, gathering the power of both.
5. **Inner reading.** From Jung onward, many read the operations as stages of psychological transformation.

## Quotations
- "That which is below is like that which is above, and that which is above is like that which is below, to do the miracles of one only thing." (Newton trans.)
- Hermes is called Trismegistus, "having the three parts of the philosophy of the whole world".
